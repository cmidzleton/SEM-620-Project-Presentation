## B. Must fix: wrong or unsupported claims

1. **Slide 9 headline borrows the wrong evidence.** "The same score has ranked bounces every year since 2016" is about the bounce score, not the empty-visit score. I ran the empty-visit score itself: last year's rate ranks next year's empty visits with **AUC 0.86 to 0.92 every year 2017-2026** (2026: 0.86). Use that. It's stronger and it's the right test. The bounce AUC (0.82 vs 0.69 logistic) can stay as a footnote.
2. **Slide 6 formula doesn't match A1.** The slide says `(empty outcomes + 10 x 5.9%) / (referrals + 10)`. The model actually scores field and desk separately with **2025** priors (field 6.6%, desk 0.8% without duplicates) and adds them. 5.9% is the 2026 rate, which the model never sees. Use: `risk = (empty visits + 10 x 6.6%) / (referrals + 10) + the same for desk outcomes`, or simplify the model to field only and say so.
3. **Slide 2, "the officer arrived and found nothing" (12,583).** The five labels include "Unable to Contact Complainant" (544 in 2025) and "Location Not Found". These don't prove a visit happened. Say "ended with nothing for the City to act on", or footnote it.
4. **Slide 2, the screen shows 28%.** That's the 2026 rate (1,350 of 4,877). The card would show last year's rate: Untidy Property was **30% in 2025 (1,493 of 4,988)**. It stays red, only just.
5. **Slide 1 subtitle.** "A 20-second check on the one in ten that end with nothing" says the check targets the bad calls, but it targets flagged codes (about 1 call in 9). Suggest: "a 20-second check on the codes that most often end with nothing."
6. **Slide 3 says 1 of 1,917 codes; A1 says 1,930.** Pick one and footnote it. Slide 16 calls the gap "where the AI was wrong", but it's a definition difference, not an error. Pick a real AI error instead.
7. **Slide 16 "six weeks" story** must change to "about a month" (see A). Also, "610 rows, 369 verified..." comes from a Nov 1 report I can't see. Keep it only if a team member can produce it.
