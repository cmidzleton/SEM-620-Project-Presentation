THE ALGORITHM · SIMPLE ON PURPOSE
The algorithm is one number per code: how often it ended with nothing last year. A rule turns it into three colours; the agent does the rest.
THE WHOLE MODEL · EXPLAINABLE IN ONE SENTENCE
risk(code) = (empty outcomes + 10 x 5.9%) / (referrals + 10)
Counted over the last 12 months of closed phone referrals, refreshed weekly. "This code ended with nothing to act on 1,350 of 4,877 times last year" is the whole explanation an agent needs. Rare codes are pulled toward the average so a code used five times cannot go red on one closure. New codes sit amber for 90 days.
WHAT KIND OF SYSTEM, AND THE QUESTION EACH KIND RAISES
KIND (SESSION 1) | IN REFERRAL CHECK | THE QUESTION TO ASK, AND OUR ANSWER
Learns a predictive pattern | The ratio per code, from past closures | How were the examples labelled? By the receiving area's closure reason, so the label is the area's verdict. Build a required reason field; audit a sample.
Applies an explicit rule | 20% and 30% cut-offs; amber for new codes | Who set the conditions and exceptions? 311 with the three pilot areas and CSU 52; reviewed at the gate.
Generates content | Nothing. No text is written or read by a model in phase one | Is it faithful to the source? Not triggered until phase two, when the Generative AI Standard's human-review rule applies.

WHY 20%, WHEN THE DECISION TREE SAYS 2%
Rolled back right to left, a 20-second check pays whenever a code's risk is above about 2%: $1.02 against 35% of a $137 visit. That is most referrals. AUTOMATE VS LEAVE TO A PERSON · AND WHEN IT IS WRONG
Automate the counting and the reminder. Never the decision to refer, close or decline: those stay with people who can be asked why.
STEP | AUTOMATED | A PERSON | WHY
Score and tier every pick | Yes: one ratio, one rule, weekly | Sets the thresholds; reviews at the gate | Counting is what machines do well; the cut-off is a value choice
Show the screening questions | Yes: the card appears on amber and red | The area writes them; the agent asks them | Only the agent hears the caller (Northfield: the interface shapes the outcome)
Decide to refer, inform or switch code | No | The agent, every time; one-click override, logged | Decision authority stays where the information is; overrides are a signal, not a fault
Close a ticket or declare no offence | No, ever | The receiving area | Accountability sits with the area that must explain the result
Retire, merge or reword a code | No: the queue is automatic, the decision is not | 311 knowledge team with the owning area | Political, not statistical: areas sign off scripts today
Coach an agent | No: the digest is coaching material only | Team lead | Feedback works from a person the agent trusts; agreed in writing with CSU 52
FOUR QUESTIONS FOR ANY AGENT · ACCESS · CHANGE · STOP · CORRECT
Access: code, closure and referral tables in the CRM; no caller notes, no names. Change: nothing; it displays and logs. Stop: tiers fall back to green if override rate passes 80%, recording drops in a pilot area, or the service is down; agents route as today. Correct: the weekly fix queue, owned jointly; an agent can flag a wrong tier from the card.
WHEN IT IS WRONG · FIVE WAYS, FIVE ANSWERS
FALSE ALARM
A red flag on a real offence costs 20 to 40 seconds and one click. The override feeds the table; a code that is overridden 80% of the time is noise and goes green.
MISSED ONE
A green code sends an officer to nothing: the same as today. The closure reason now returns within a day and the code's ratio moves.
DRIFT
An area changes how it closes tickets (eight began recording bounces in 2026). Track recording rate by area; judge the pilot only against its control group.
RUBBER STAMP
Agents accept every prompt: audit a sample when acceptance passes 95%. Answer-first design: the questions, never a pre-selected outcome.
TURNED INTO A GATE
The Robodebt failure: a proxy becomes a reason to refuse service and the burden shifts to the resident. Rule: the algorithm never declines a referral; the caller can always insist; "referrals declined without consent" is measured and must read zero.

THE LENS · RATIONAL, PSYCHOLOGICAL, POLITICAL
Each lens changes one design choice: the rational lens sets the floor, the psychological lens sets the cut-off, the political lens sets the order of asking.
LOGICAL LAUREN  ·  RATIONAL
The arithmetic says check almost everything. I set the floor, not the ceiling.
EXPECTED VALUE
A 20-second check pays above about 2% risk; rolled back, $375K a year across the 150 flagged codes. An average, not a forecast.
LOSS BESIDE THE AVERAGE
P10 is $171K; no draw is negative. The loss case is not money, it is 20 seconds on 38,000 calls and a prompt agents stop reading.
OPTION VALUE OF WAITING
Waiting for the 2030 CRM costs three years of freed capacity (about $1.1M) and arrives with no labelled data. Waiting has a price; we state it.
DOING NOTHING, STAGED OPTIONS
The base case is on slide 14. The pilot is the small, staged option; phase two and three are bought only if the gate passes.
NEURO NAV  ·  PSYCHOLOGICAL
Agents do not need to be more careful. They need one concrete question and to hear how it turned out.
ATTENTION
"Be more thoughtful" does nothing on a 120-second clock. The card gives one manageable question on one call in five, and the clock pauses for flagged calls.
FEEDBACK FOR INTUITION
Kahneman and Klein: intuition needs feedback that links action to result. Today a closure reason never reaches the agent; the digest returns it within a day.
ANCHORING
A suggested outcome would anchor. The card shows the rate and the questions, never a pre-selected answer; the agent writes the answer.
GAIN, LOSS, REFERENCE POINT
Reference point is speed. Frame what agents gain (officers not sent to nothing, a fair measure) and name the loss (20 seconds). Calling concern "resistance" answers nothing.
RULING RICK  ·  POLITICAL
The ratio is harmless. Publishing it is political: it makes the areas' closures visible for the first time.
THE PLAYERS
311 owns the pick; areas own the outcome labels and sign off scripts; CSU 52 represents the agents; Council asked for closure and duplicate numbers, not misroutes; the Auditor closed all five recommendations.
WHERE YOU STAND

BIAS | WHAT HAPPENS (THE SITUATION) | WHAT IS NEGLECTED | WHAT THE DESIGN DOES
Availability | A memorable empty visit (or a memorable real offence) stands in for how often each happens | The ordinary, unmemorable cases: 4,877 untidy-property referrals a year | The ratio counts every case; the card shows the rate, not an anecdote

WHAT THE HISTORY ADDS
The bounce rate stepped up in 2022 and 2026; both steps line up with more areas recording (all-channel bounces 2,162 in 2021 to 4,515 in 2022). The count tracks recording as much as coding.
Phone volume fell by two thirds (1.83M phone tickets in 2016 to 0.67M in 2025) while phone codes grew from 1,701 to 1,930: less practice per code each year, which is the Kahneman and Klein condition eroding.
One-year rolling history is enough and adapts faster to recording changes.
