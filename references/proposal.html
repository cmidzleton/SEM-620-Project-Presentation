City of Edmonton 311: service request classification and routing

Client 311 and Customer Access, City of Edmonton Team Team 2 Source Team 2 Project Proposal 1.pdf (2 pages)

The organization

Edmonton 311 is the City's non-emergency contact centre, staffed by more than 140 representatives. The City publishes a live 311 request dataset (673,101 requests at retrieval) and a 2023 City Auditor report on 311. The team is local to Edmonton, which makes primary research practical.

Core analytic decision, as proposed
When a request comes in, which category and program code does it get, and does 311 resolve it directly or refer it to the responsible business area?

Scale, as cited in the proposal

6.5Mtickets created by 311 agents, 2018 to 2022 (about 1.3M a year)
73%of 2018 to 2022 tickets were full service (resolved on the call)
$14.66M2026 budget for 311 and Customer Access
$16.29cost per inquiry ($14.66M ÷ 900,000 inquiries)

The five-part test

Test questionFinding
1. Who makes it, and how often?A 311 agent, one of 140+ representatives. Agents created 6.5M tickets from 2018 to 2022. In 2026 to date, 311 has closed 662,473 tickets and has 12,017 open. The City handles about 900,000 inquiries a year through 311.
2. What inputs are used now?The citizen's description of the problem and its location, checked against scripts supplied by each business area. Agents may ask clarifying questions before assigning a program code and ticket type.
3. What are the options?Resolve the request directly, refer it to the responsible business area, or create and close the ticket when the request is transferred. Full service was 73% of tickets, 2018 to 2022.
4. What does a bad instance cost?The Auditor did not cost a ticket. From 2018 to 2022 there were about 79,000 duplicate and 52,000 follow-up tickets. At $16.29 a call, duplicates could cost about $1.29M and follow-ups about $0.85M, over $2.1M in total. Unresolved concerns can escalate to Councillors or the Mayor. Toronto's 311 costs $11 to $16 a call.
5. Could an algorithm make it instead?Yes. A peer-reviewed study classified 311 requests from citizen descriptions with over 83% accuracy (Hashemi, 2022). Edmonton added an AI feature in July 2026 that suggests a service category from a photo or description; its role in routing was still to be confirmed.

What the October deck changed

  • Decision narrowed to which service code a phone or web ticket gets (1 of 1,917 phone codes), because the code sets both the receiving area and resolve-or-refer on 99.4% of phone tickets.
  • Duplicates dropped from scope. Council's July 2025 motion already targets them and the City auto-links about 1 in 3 at intake.
  • Stake revised from $2.1M to about $100K a year (Monte Carlo, P10 to P90 $74K to $131K). The proposal's figure counted every duplicate and follow-up, most of which no code caused.
  • $16.29 per contact replaced with 2025 budget figures ($15.52 / $15.89 / $21.50), since the original mixed a 2026 budget with 2025 volumes.
  • Hashemi's 83% dropped as a human error benchmark. It is a neural network's test accuracy on nine classes in San Diego.
