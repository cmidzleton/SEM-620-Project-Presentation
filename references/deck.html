The diagnosis in one paragraph

Every 311 phone agent answers the same question on every call: which of 1,917 service codes fits the caller's words. The code decides which City area gets the work and whether 311 resolves or refers. When the code is wrong, the receiving area eventually closes the ticket as "311 Redirecting to Other Business Area" (a bounce), a median 1.6 days later. Nothing the team found tells the agent, no published measure counts it, and authority (311 picks the code) sits apart from accountability (the area owns the result). The deck is a diagnosis only and explicitly defers the solution to December.

Core analytic decision, refined
Which service code does a new 311 phone or web ticket get?
In scope: 665,225 phone + 29,911 web tickets (2025). Out of scope: app tickets (resident picks from 77 options, AI suggests since Jul 2026) and duplicate linking (already on Council's agenda).

What this deck does not yet cover for the final presentation

The final brief asks for a full proposal. This deck stops at diagnosis, so these parts are still to build:

  • The algorithmic routine: inputs, outputs, who acts on it, what happens when it is wrong, and what is automated vs left to the agent. The deck's own findings point at candidates: a code suggestion on the agent's screen, an "ask the owner" prompt on high-bounce codes, and a bounce feedback loop back to the agent.
  • Data construction: what exists (open data q7ua-agfg, scripts), what does not (agent's first code, link from bounce to re-route, quiet misroutes), and what would have to be created.
  • Integration and phasing: a 90-day plan, phase-two gates, and success measures. The deck flags the CRM's 2030 end of life and the CSU 52 / agent-replacement concern as constraints.
  • Open questions with the City: three questions sent via Open Data; no substantive answers as of Oct 22, 2026.

The problem in brief

A wrong code on a phone or web ticket sends work to the wrong City area. It costs about $100K a year; no published measure tracks it

  • Phone 665,225 tickets (agent picks from 1,917 codes); web 29,911 (form or map sets the code by rule, staff code emails).
  • Visible: 1,323 bounces in 2025. Not visible: quiet fixes inside areas and wrong answers among 473,564 phone-resolved calls.
  • Introduces the three characters, the call bar, and the evidence labels.
3
Caller 4's ticket, Monday to Thursday

One wrong code cost Caller 4 two days in the wrong queue and a second call, and nothing tells the agent who picked it

  • Timeline: call 9:05, code picked 9:06, bounced Wednesday, re-route not public, second call Thursday.
  • 2025 phone: 1,128 bounces; 22.3% waited over 7 days; 14,272 days summed in the wrong queue; 61 of 116 tickets on the picked code closed as not theirs.
  • Per service day: ~2,150 codes picked, ~4 bounced, 46 days added to wrong queues.
4
The decision, named · five-part test

The decision passes all five tests: on a call, a 311 agent picks 1 of 1,917 codes

  • Who/how often: 140+ agents, 665,225 picks a year. Inputs: caller's words and address, code list, ~900 scripts, experience.
  • Options: 1,917 codes, each fixing receiving area and (99.4%) resolve-or-refer. Cost: ~$31 a bounce before field visits; ~$100K a year.
  • Could a model do it: plausibly; the City's app AI already suggests a service from words or a photo.
  • Scope: phone and web in; app, duplicate linking and any solution out.
5
Why this decision · 11 candidates, 9 criteria

Phone coding is the only one of 11 at full grain and frequency: third on team weights, first of 311's own decisions, 7th blind

  • Weighted score 67 (bylaw triage 72 and pothole plan 71 lead but are decided outside 311).
  • Scores 1 on value and payback, 5 on grain and frequency. Duplicate linking not scored (already underway).
  • A blind AI re-score ranked it 7th of 12; the team calls the pick a judgment and says so.
6
What the City automated, and what it left

The City added AI to the app, where residents pick. On the phone (74% of tickets), agents pick with no suggestion found

  • App (205,298 tickets): AI suggests a service since Jul 14, 2026, "to reduce incorrect assignments"; performance unpublished.
  • Underway: AI search over ~900 scripts for agents, framed around shorter calls. "AI backends to help direct callers" is a reporter's paraphrase.
  • Left to people: the phone stays "the primary access point". Framed with Reassembly's configuration, embedding, performance.
7
What the bounce count shows, 2023 to 2026

The 2026 rise in bounces is no clear sign of worse coding: 39% is new recording, 52% is areas back at their 2023 rate

  • Phone bounces per referral, Jan to Sep: 0.57% (2023), 0.48%, 0.44%, 1.03% (2026). Fall 2025: City "aligned ticket closure processes".
  • Phone bounces Jan to Sep rose 661 to 1,673; eight rarely-recording areas went 8 to 398.
  • The can code fails on both channels in 2026: 37 of 78 phone, 469 of 645 app. The problem is in the code set, not only who picks.
8
Problem tree, by stage of one call

Wrong codes start before and during the call; after it, no published measure counts them and nothing tells the agent

  • Before: 1,917 codes; areas sign off scripts; ownership follows the asset, not the problem.
  • During: caller's words rarely name the owner; rare codes bounce 14x more (3.80% vs 0.27%) but are 5% of bounces; speed incentives (120 s answer).
  • After: no count, no word back, no link from bounce to re-route. Stated assumption: a bounce is treated as a coding error.
9
The decision today · six activities, five conditions

One pick records the request, proposes the action and commits the route; no second check before routing and no feedback after

  • Notice, Record, Propose, Authorize, Carry out, Learn: Authorize and Learn are the gaps.
  • Conditions for meaningful review: evidence ✕, expertise ~, time ✕, authority ~, feedback ~.
  • 311 holds authority over the code; the area holds accountability for the result; no owner of the hand-off.
10
The stake · profit tree

Wrong codes on phone and web tickets cost the City about $100K a year; only $47K comes from tickets the open data counts

  • Counted $46.6K (bounces $40.4K, wrong jurisdiction $5.6K, "more info" $0.6K) + unrecorded $11.5K + knock-on $42.4K (visits $13.4K, repeat calls $29.0K).
  • 13 triangular inputs, 50,000 Monte Carlo draws, seed 20261018. All at most likely: $69.4K.
  • Biggest swing input: wrong answers caused by the code (team judgment). Who pays: 311 ~60%, areas ~40%.
11
Logical Lauren · decision trees

Wrong codes cost about $0.14 a ticket. Asking the owner first pays only on often-bounced codes; nothing tells the agent which

  • EV of one ticket today: -$0.1449; × 693,357 priced tickets = -$100.5K, reconciling to the profit tree.
  • Pick now vs ask first (20 s, $1.02): asking pays when bounce probability p > 5.6%.
  • Average referral p 0.55% favours picking; the can code p 43% favours asking. The agent never sees p.
12
Neuro Nav · one call as the agent meets it

A wrong code can still feel right: no word back to the agent, and 1,366 of 1,917 codes come up fewer than 100 times a year

  • Glaser's five questions applied to one call; System 1 offers a can code before the owner is asked.
  • Bounce rate by code use: 3.80% (1-9), 1.96% (10-99), 0.64% (100-999), 0.27% (1,000+). Correlation only.
  • Situation / tendency / neglected table for agents, 311 leaders and receiving areas. Hypotheses H1 to H7.
13
Ruling Rick · the players

311 picks the code; the business areas sign off the scripts and own the result; no published scorecard counts a wrong pick

  • Player table: agents, 311 management, business areas, City Auditor, residents, CSU 52, each with measures, moves, stakes.
  • Council asked about closures, duplicates, compliance; the Sep 2026 report gave 98.7% / 81% / 94%, no bounce count.
  • Sedimentation: 2023 audit called full-service tickets "low risk"; 2026 says "efficiently routed" with no routing measure. The 2030 CRM replacement is where ownership will be settled.
14
The full picture · 2026 pace, residents, hazards

At the 2026 pace the City cost would be $139K to $154K a year: recorded bounces more than doubled, even if errors did not

  • Residents: 16,987 days in the wrong queue in 2025; about $8K of residents' time on 2,017 extra contacts.
  • Hazards: 231 hazard-type phone tickets bounced, 2,915 days in the wrong queue. Alberta MGA s. 532(6) liability question left open.
  • At marginal agent time only, the 2025 stake is $61K.
15
Shift debrief

One choice, made about 2,150 times a day on the phone: no published measure, no word back and no owner of the miss

  • Lauren: what it costs. Nav: why it repeats. Rick: why it is uncounted.
  • Unknowns: does the code call up the script; how often resolved calls got the wrong code; how bounces are re-routed; who adds or retires codes.
  • Questions for the City: 311 team lead, area dispatcher, 311 agent.
16
Why the City has not measured it yet

No published measure counts wrong codes: the data shows only recorded bounces, ownership is split, scorecards count speed

  • Data built to assign work, not count misses. Ownership "shared by everyone, owned by no one".
  • Espoused access vs enacted efficiency. Updates, not learning: every recent fix targets other problems.
  • Framed as not concealment: the City publishes every recorded bounce. CRM end of life in 2030.
17
AI process reflection

AI's first answers were fast and fluent, like System 1. A self-check can defend them, so independent checks decided what we believed

  • Tools: Claude (Opus, Fable, Sonnet/Haiku), Gemini Pro; critic agents, LLM council, blind re-score, deck analyzer, HTML keep/cut tool.
  • Human-in-the-loop: the team found the City already working on duplicates and steered AI to ticket coding.
  • Errors caught: 24/7 line assumption, misread benchmarks, reporter paraphrase treated as quote, biased re-score; four cost models reconciled with a Monte Carlo.
