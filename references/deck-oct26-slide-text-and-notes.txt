Out of scope: app tickets (the resident picks the service from 77 options with an AI suggestion), duplicate linking (Council's July 2, 2025 motion asked for progress on duplicate tickets, and the City links about 1 in 3 duplicates automatically at intake), and anything the City should do about it (diagnosis only).

NOTES: SLIDE 5. Why this decision, after the team's slide. Eleven candidate decisions were scored on nine weighted criteria in the team's alternatives work (team judgment, scores unchanged): grain 15, frequency 10, value at stake 15, payback 5, data available 10, feasibility 10, not already done 20, depth of the human story 10, risk 5. Score = sum of (score x weight) / 5; every row recomputes (phone coding: (5x15 + 5x10 + 1x15 + 1x5 + 3x10 + 4x10 + 3x20 + 4x10 + 4x5) / 5 = 67).
Gate, applied first: does 311 make this decision? 311 is the client named in our proposal. Bylaw triage (72) and the pothole crew plan (71) score higher but are decided by Community Standards and by Parks and Roads crews. Among the decisions 311 makes, phone coding leads incomplete app reports (65) by 2 points. All 11 candidates, the anchors and the robustness checks are in Appendix D; the evidence for every score is in Appendix D2; the blind re-score is in Appendix D3.
Not scored: duplicate linking. It is a 311 decision, but Council asked for progress on "addressing duplicate tickets" (July 2, 2025 motion, CSD03148 p. 1) and the City already links part of it automatically ("Technology enhancements now link duplicate requests to the original ticket", p. 2): in 2025, 11,510 of 34,239 duplicate closures were linked within 5 minutes of creation (Q11). The brief warns against a decision the organization is visibly already working on. A blind re-score by a fresh AI session (Appendix D3) ranked phone coding 7th of twelve (61): the four leaders are decided outside 311, and two of 311's own decisions (intake screening, incomplete app reports) scored 63. We treat our pick as a judgment and say so.
Value at stake: the stake on slide 10 (mean about $100K a year for 2025; $139K-$154K at the 2026 pace) is still under the $0.25M anchor for a score of 1.

NOTES: SLIDE 7. What the bounce count does and does not show. The chart is phone and app bounces per 100 referrals by month, January 2023 to September 2026 (October 2026 is partial and left out). Both channels rise and fall together and both jumped in fall 2025, when the City "aligned ticket closure processes with work completion for several outstanding services" (CSD03148 Att. 1 p. 2).
Decomposing the rise (phone referral bounces, Jan-Sep): 661 in 2025, 1,673 in 2026, +1,012. Eight areas that recorded almost no bounces before 2026 (Development Permits, Municipal Enforcement, Development Compliance, Development Services, Peace Officers, Park Rangers, Parking Enforcement, Fire Services) went from 8 to 398: 390, or 39% of the rise. Two of them, Development Permits and Development Compliance, were created in March 2026 and carry 220 of the 398. The five largest areas that recorded every year (Roadway Maintenance, Parks, Forestry, Traffic, Building and Mechanical) went from 533 to 1,059: 526, or 52%; their pooled rate is 1.86% (2023), 1.74%, 1.12%, 1.96% (2026), so together they are back at 2023. Area by area (2023 to 2026): Roadway Maintenance 2.34% to 1.74%; Parks 6.80% to 8.04%; Forestry 0.71% to 1.50%; Traffic 0.67% to 1.30%; Building and Mechanical 0.13% to 1.14%. Four of the five rose above their own 2023 rate, so part of the 52% may be real change or a change in how those areas record; we cannot tell which from public data. METHOD AND CHECKS
Each input is a triangular "fuzzy number" (min, most likely, max). A draw takes u from U(0,1) and inverts the triangular distribution; all 13 inputs are drawn together, 50,000 times (seed 20261018).
The tree shows each line's mean, so the boxes add; P10 and P90 are the 10th and 90th percentiles of each line and of the total.
The repeat-call cap (half of 11,980 follow-ups and escalations, CSD03148) binds in 0.3% of draws.
Who pays: 311 the contact costs (60%); the receiving areas desk time and visits.
